## What works
- **Exploiting kernel/data symmetry:** Identifying mathematical symmetries in computation structures and using algebraic identities to reduce operation count while preserving exact results. The inline-symmetric-convolutions idea pre-combined symmetric neighbor pixels (e.g., `N0-N2-N6+N8`) and multiplied by fewer unique kernel weights, reducing 36 multiplications to 16 (~11.7% user time improvement).
- **Eliminating intermediate arrays:** Removing small stack-allocated arrays (kernel matrices, neighbour_pixel arrays) in favor of direct inline computation reduces stack memory traffic and register pressure. Confirmed by inline-symmetric-convolutions eliminating 9-element `kern_*` arrays.
- **Mathematical simplifications:** Pure algebraic reductions that provably produce identical floating-point results (e.g., removing `0.5f` scaling factors that cancel out, computing `cos²θ`/`sin²θ` directly from gradients instead of computing angles).
- **Judicious loop pairing/unrolling:** Combining loops that do similar, independent work over the same dimensions helps vectorization and ILP. Note: this means running operations side-by-side, NOT mathematically fusing them (see "what to avoid").

## What to avoid
- **Changing floating-point evaluation order:** Pre-combining or rearranging multiply-accumulate operations — even when mathematically equivalent — changes rounding sequences and causes benchmark errors. The fuse-paired-kernel-convolutions idea (combining `kern_first[k]*ABCD[0] + kern_second[k]*ABCD[1]` before convolution) failed this way.
- **Conditional branching to skip work:** Adding conditionals to skip computation consistently fails. skip-zero-speed-orders caused benchmark errors even though the check was outside the pixel loop (once per scale). In inner loops, branching also destroys SIMD vectorization and pipeline predictability.
- **Over-merging loops:** Fusing fundamentally different computational stages (e.g., gradient computation with sums/variance) causes register spilling, breaks auto-vectorization, or destroys cache locality.
- **Fast-math/precision reductions:** Lower-precision intrinsics (`dt_fast_inv_sqrtf`, `native_rsqrt`) or `#pragma GCC optimize("fast-math")` cause regression test failures due to the strict numerical precision required by the PDE diffusion model.
