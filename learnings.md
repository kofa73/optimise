## What works
- **Exploiting kernel/data symmetry to reduce operation count:** Identifying mathematical symmetries and using algebraic identities to eliminate large fractions of work. `inline-symmetric-convolutions` pre-combined symmetric neighbor pixels and reduced 36 multiplications to 16 per channel (~11.7% improvement).
- **Replacing temporary arrays with scalar variables:** `strip-internal-array-allocations-for-find_gradients-normalizations` stripped internal array allocations into native scalars, yielding ~11.9% improvement. The pattern: find small fixed-size arrays used as intermediaries, replace with individual scalar variables to help the compiler use registers.
- **Targeting the largest time consumers:** Both successful optimizations targeted the inner per-pixel loop where small per-pixel savings multiply across millions of pixels.
- **Preserving identical floating-point evaluation order:** Successful optimizations must produce bit-identical results by not reordering multiply-accumulate sequences. This is a strict prerequisite for passing benchmarks.

## What to avoid
- **Inlining non-local pixel access:** Eliminating stack-allocated neighbor arrays in favor of direct memory fetches causes obvious regressions (+4.0% regression for `inline-neighbor-pixel-access`). Bulk copying to stack arrays is faster than repeated reads from L1 cache.
- **Over-merging loops / pairing function calls:** Grouping `compute_convolution` calls into fewer shared loops causes massive regressions (-48.7% for `pair-4-compute_convolution-calls-into-2-for_each_channel-loops`), likely due to increased loop overhead and register pressure.
- **Changing floating-point evaluation order:** Pre-combining or rearranging multiply-accumulate operations changes rounding sequences and causes benchmark errors. `fuse-paired-kernel-convolutions` failed by mathematically combining kernels before convolution.
- **Restructuring code without algorithmic reduction:** Fusing operations without reducing the total operation count hurts rather than helps. Fusing angle and exponential computations (`Fused-angle-ex`) caused an 8.1% regression.
- **Conditional branching to skip work inside tight loops:** Adding conditionals to skip computations (even expensive ones like `expf`) causes regressions or minimal improvements that don't justify the branch overhead. `copy-matched-anisotropy-c2-to-skip-expf` gave a regression (-1.1%), likely because branching disrupted vectorization or pipelining.
- **Micro-optimizations that disrupt the compiler:** Simple substitutions (like scalar gradient fetching optimization) are often unhelpful or not applicable to the compiled output, as modern compilers already optimize them well.
